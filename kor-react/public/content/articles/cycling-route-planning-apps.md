# Best Cycling Route Planners: Strava, Komoot, RWGPS & More

The best cycling route planner is the one you'll actually use. Strava Routes, Komoot, and Ride with GPS each solve the same core problem — getting you from A to B on roads or trails worth riding — but they approach it differently. This comparison covers features, pricing, surface-type data, and which app works best for different types of riders.

## The Four Main Options

Four apps dominate cycling route planning. Each has a niche, and understanding where they differ helps you commit to one rather than bounce between four.

| App | Best For | Free Tier | Premium Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| Strava Routes | Strava users, heatmap routing | Yes (limited) | $11.99/mo or $79.99/yr |
| Komoot | Gravel, MTB, surface-type data | Yes (one region) | €29.99/yr |
| Ride with GPS | Touring, cue sheets, club events | Yes (limited) | $9.99/mo or $69.99/yr |
| Garmin Connect / Wahoo | Head-unit-first, device ecosystems | Yes | Device subscription |

---

## Strava Routes

Strava Routes uses aggregated GPS data — the heatmap — to suggest routes based on where other cyclists actually ride. The more Strava users in your area, the better the suggestions. On busy roads and popular trails, it works well. In low-density rural areas, the heatmap can be thin and the suggestions suffer for it.

**What it does well:**

- Heatmap routing shows roads and trails cyclists prefer, not just what maps list
- Automatic elevation profiles and KOM/QOM segment matching
- One-tap sync to Garmin, Wahoo, and Hammerhead head units via Connected Partners
- Stays inside the Strava ecosystem — no separate login or app to manage

**Free vs. paid:**
Route creation is available on the free tier, but live route data on your phone and matched segment notifications require a paid Strava subscription ($11.99/month, $79.99/year).

**Surface type data:** Basic. Strava doesn't break out surface type explicitly — you infer it from heatmap density and activity type filters (rides vs. runs vs. walking). Not ideal for gravel or mixed-surface planning.

---

## Komoot

Komoot is built around outdoor adventure across disciplines: road cycling, gravel, mountain bike, hiking, and running. Its clearest differentiator is surface-type data — it tells you what percentage of a route is paved, unpaved, singletrack, or road before you ride it. That detail matters if you're on a gravel bike, planning a loaded tour, or scouting a new trail.

**What it does well:**

- Surface type breakdown per route — rare at this price
- Curated highlights and community waypoints at key points
- Offline maps — essential for remote routes where you lose signal
- Strong European coverage; solid and improving elsewhere

**Pricing:**
The free tier includes one region of your choice (a roughly state-sized area). Routing across regions requires buying individual regions (~€3.99 each) or the All-Mountain Adventures bundle (€29.99/year) for worldwide access.

**Head unit sync:** Syncs directly to Garmin and Wahoo via connected accounts. Routes export as GPX for any other device.

## Ride with GPS

Ride with GPS (RWGPS) is the pick for structured riding: touring, centuries, club events, and any route where cue sheets and turn-by-turn navigation matter. It has the most granular route customization of the three main options, and it's the only one with a built-in cue sheet editor that produces the kind of printed turn list a long-distance cyclist actually needs.

**What it does well:**

- Best cue sheet and turn-by-turn tools in the category — period
- Community route library with thousands of shared routes
- Map layers including satellite, topo, and surface overlays
- Club and event accounts make it easy to share routes with groups

**Pricing:**
The free tier allows route creation and saving but limits GPS file downloads and advanced features. Basic plan runs $9.99/month ($69.99/year).

**Surface type data:** Good. RWGPS map layers show surface type, and during route creation you can prefer or avoid unpaved segments — useful when planning gravel-to-road ratios.

## Garmin Connect and Wahoo ELEMNT Companion

If you already use a Garmin or Wahoo head unit, their native apps are worth knowing — they're designed to push routes to your device with minimal steps.

**Garmin Connect:**

- Courses built in Garmin Connect push directly to your Edge via WiFi or Bluetooth sync
- Garmin's Trendline Popularity Routing is licensed Strava heatmap data, so the suggestions are comparable to Strava Routes
- Best choice if you're fully in the Garmin ecosystem and don't want another app

**Wahoo ELEMNT Companion:**

- Route creation is basic — better used as an importer than a planner
- Accepts GPX from any app, so the common workflow is: plan in Komoot or RWGPS, export GPX, import to Wahoo
- SYSTM integration adds training plan context but isn't a standalone route planner

## How to Pick the Right App

**You're already on Strava and ride popular roads or trails** → Strava Routes. The heatmap routing is good enough for most riding, you're already on the platform, and the head unit sync is seamless.

**You ride gravel or mountain bike and care about surface type** → Komoot. The surface breakdown per route is the feature that matters here. Buy the worldwide bundle if you travel to ride.

**You're touring, doing long-distance events, or need printable cue sheets** → Ride with GPS. The turn-by-turn and cue sheet tools are in a different league.

**You're device-first on Garmin** → Build in Garmin Connect or import GPX from Komoot or RWGPS. Trendline routing covers most everyday rides.

Most riders end up using two: a primary planner for serious routes, and Strava Routes for quick rides where they want to follow where other cyclists go.

## Exporting Routes to Your Head Unit

All four apps support GPX export — the universal format for cycling GPS files. The process is the same regardless of which app you use:

1. **Build the route** in your planner of choice
2. **Export as GPX** (look for a download or share button)
3. **Transfer to your device:**
   - Direct sync: Strava, Komoot, or RWGPS → Garmin or Wahoo via connected accounts
   - Manual: connect your device via USB and drop the GPX file into the Courses or NewFiles folder
4. **Navigate on-device:** the route appears in your Courses menu

For Garmin devices via USB: place GPX files in `/Garmin/NewFiles/` — the device imports them on next power-on. For Wahoo: use the ELEMNT app's Import Route feature (Settings → Routes → Import from Files).
